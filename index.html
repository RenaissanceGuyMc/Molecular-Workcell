02 / The central thesis

We can build parts.
Can we build processes?

We can fold DNA into intricate structures, design molecular machines, drive nanoscale motion, and synthesize atomically defined products in specialized settings. Yet these achievements do not automatically add up to a system that can perform reliable sequences of useful molecular operations.

Molecular Workcell Engineering asks how to create local environments where positioning, selective chemical action, measurement, and correction can be engineered together, then connected into longer workflows.

The unit of progress is not a beautiful nanoscale device. It is a verified operation whose performance survives contact with the next operation.

The current fragmentation

Components without a common engineering language.

Structural DNA nanotechnology, molecular motors, synthetic chemistry, sensing, and nanofabrication advance on separate metrics. Each has impressive demonstrations. Few experiments test their compatibility as an end-to-end process.

The proposed discipline

Functions that can be measured, compared, and composed.

Develop shared benchmarks for controlled encounters, selective action, state readout, error accounting, and multi-step reliability. Let multiple architectures compete on the same tasks, instead of presuming a single path will win.

03 / A distinct unit of engineering

The workcell
is the missing unit.

Conventional molecular research often reports what a structure is or a machine can do. Molecular Workcell Engineering asks what a complete operation does reproducibly, under specified conditions, with its failure modes accounted for.

That is a proposed common engineering discipline, not a claim that one particular scaffold or robot must win. The physical environment may do most of the work.

01 / Constrain

Make the encounter tractable.

Use fixtures, selective chemistry, interfaces, or surfaces to reduce the relevant degrees of freedom. An incoming component need not hold a precise orientation while freely diffusing.

02 / Operate

Convert state into useful work.

Carry out a bounded attachment, chemical transformation, displacement, or materialization step. Test whether the action produces the intended product rather than merely movement.

03 / Verify and decide

Measure, reject, or proceed.

Establish how the readout maps to actual outcome. Account for misattachment, incomplete transformation, and false verification before connecting the next operation.

Not a shrunken industrial robot

The tool, scaffold, energy supply, and measurement may be distributed across molecules, an instrument, and an externally controlled environment.

Not a single preferred architecture

DNA, designed proteins, synthetic motors, surfaces, scanning probes, and solution chemistry can be compared on the same verified-operation task.

04 / Research agenda

Seven fronts.
One discipline.

The field is deliberately larger than DNA origami, artificial molecular machines, or surface chemistry alone. It needs a common experimental language across physical regimes while preserving competing architectures.

The founding question: what must be measured, controlled, and reproduced before a molecular action becomes an engineering primitive?

01 / FIX↗

Programmable fixtures

Addressable geometries, confinement, local orientation, substrate interfaces, and selective attachment that increase useful encounters without requiring impossible mechanical authority.

02 / INTERFACE↗

Hybrid molecular interfaces

DNA, proteins, peptides, enzymes, and nonbiological surfaces used as interoperable structural and functional elements. Compatibility, cross-talk, and fouling are first-class variables.

03 / DRIVE↗

Energy and actuation

Chemical, electrochemical, optical, and magnetic ways to bias molecular events. The question is not whether motion occurs, but whether it contributes useful, selective work.

04 / READ↗

State sensing and metrology

Single-molecule observability, optical and spin-sensitive reporters, and calibrated inference. A signal must be causally tied to the operation it claims to verify.

05 / RECOVER↗

Error-aware operations

Misconnection, incomplete reactions, off-target events, and repair or discard pathways. Reliability must be measured through failure distributions, not best-case demonstrations.

06 / MODEL↗

Predictive design systems

Stochastic dynamics, molecular simulation, experimental design, automated analysis, and uncertainty-aware digital models that are tested against measurements rather than treated as proof.

07 / EXTEND↗

Beyond biological substrates

Explore transitions from biomolecular workcells to inorganic materials, surface-bound synthesis, and atomically defined structures. A long-term field must confront substrate transfer and industrial scale rather than assume biological scaffolds are universally suitable.

Deliberate constraint: no preferred architecture wins by definition. Solution-phase chemistry, self-assembly, and established lithography are legitimate baselines. Molecular workcells must demonstrate a capability or performance advantage worth their additional complexity.
05 / The long horizon

From programmed
molecules to
programmed matter.

Atomic precision is an ambitious target, but it is not a sensible requirement for every component. The field should choose fabrication methods by the function they deliver, the fidelity they require, and their full cost of verification.

A credible bridge to atomically precise manufacturing may be hybrid: bulk or template-directed assembly creates most of an object, while chemically selective, high-precision methods create or repair the small fraction of features that determine its performance.

Regime I

Programmable biomolecular assembly

Addressable DNA and protein structures, recognition, conformational logic, and selective catalysis.

Regime II

Bio-templated materials

Transfer encoded geometry or function into inorganic material through selected deposition and growth processes.

Regime III

Hybrid precision fabrication

Combine molecular templates with surfaces, lithography, field control, and site-specific processing.

Regime IV

Atomically selective operations

Explore positional chemistry where exact local composition or bonding is indispensable, subject to throughput and metrology constraints.

The economic test: increase precision only when the resulting functional advantage exceeds the penalties of yield loss, instrumentation, precursor preparation, contamination control, and verification. General-purpose molecular manufacturing remains unproven, and these regimes are not stages every technology must traverse.

06 / What would count as proof?

One operation.
Fully accounted for.

The first common challenge should be deceptively simple: bring a component to an intended site, direct a selective transformation or attachment, and independently verify what happened. Then run enough trials to characterize the errors.

A second test asks whether the verified output can become the input to another operation without sacrificing reliability.

A proposed Workcell Benchmark

Measures, not promises
01 / Outcome
Correct-operation yield

Fraction of attempts producing the intended outcome, with uncertainty and independent confirmation.

02 / Selectivity
Off-target events

Wrong binding, unintended chemistry, interference, and failure to reject near-matches.

03 / Measurement
Verification fidelity

False-positive and false-negative rates; correspondence between reporter signal and ground truth.

04 / Dynamics
Time and throughput

Operation cycle time, usable event rates, and the dependence on conditions and scale.

05 / Resources
Energy and cost

Energy, reagents, instrumentation, and effective cost per verified successful operation.

06 / Scale
Compositional reliability

How performance changes after operations are connected and independently replicated.

Why this matters: with independent uncorrected steps of 99.9% success, the probability of a flawless 1,000-step sequence is 0.999^1000 ≈ 36.8%. Correlated errors may be worse. Verification, recovery, and alternative process designs are not optional extras.
GATE / 01

Selective event

Define an operation and demonstrate it under measured conditions.

GATE / 02

Independent replication

Separate teams reproduce the operation and its error profile.

GATE / 03

Verified state

Show a readout with known sensitivity, specificity, and measurement cost.

GATE / 04

Composable sequence

Connect distinct steps and quantify the cumulative failure rate.

GATE / 05

Useful task

Beat a conventional alternative in a well-defined application.

07 / Reasons this could fail

Make the hard
problems public.

The field should be organized around problems capable of killing its own thesis, rather than protected by an ever-expanding technology roadmap. A candidate workcell must outperform the relevant simpler alternative.

Three questions are especially decisive. The technical companion lays out eleven testable bottlenecks and proposed acceptance criteria.

B02 / Docking

Did the intended attachment actually happen?

Correctly docked, misattached, and unattached states can be indistinguishable in a conventional bulk measurement.

Evidence required: independently calibrated readout with misattachment and false-positive rates reported per operation.
B06 / Metrology

Can verification scale with fabrication?

A process that requires bespoke microscopy for every object may never be economically useful even when its chemistry works.

Evidence required: lower-cost or parallel readout validated against an independent measurement with defined detection limits.
B11 / Generality

Do the engineering rules actually transfer?

If every machine needs new bespoke rules, there may be no coherent field or shared design system, only a loose collection of devices.

Evidence required: a predeclared rule improves first-pass functional performance in a second, genuinely different machine class.

08 / The field-building horizon

Build the field,
not just a lab.

A nascent field needs more than inspiring devices. It needs a shared definition of progress, a place to publish negative results, new collaborations, and the beginnings of a durable funding and training structure.

These are proposed milestones, not existing programs or guaranteed outcomes.

YEAR 01 / CONVENE

Define the first trials

Publish a short technical manifesto and a versioned benchmark; award small exploratory grants; host an open methods workshop across molecular machinery, nanometrology, simulation, and chemistry.

YEAR 02 / REPLICATE

Turn demonstrations into datasets

Run multi-lab round-robin tests; release baseline measurements and negative results; form cross-disciplinary teams around operations instead of material platforms.

YEAR 03 / COMPOSE

Make interoperability a research target

Fund experiments that connect separately developed fixtures, actuation, chemistry, and state readout; publish performance failures as well as successes.

YEAR 04 / INSTITUTIONALIZE

Create a recognizable research home

Establish a recurring Molecular Workcell Trials meeting, interdisciplinary training material, collaborative funding lines, and a dedicated journal collection or proceedings track.

YEAR 05 / TEST UTILITY

Show which operations matter

Demonstrate independently reproduced multi-step processes, evaluate manufacturability, and identify the applications where workcells outperform simpler methods. Retire architectures that do not.

09 / Scientific basis

Ambition should
survive evidence.

Workcell Engineering is a proposed integration discipline. The individual technical pillars are real; their reliable and economical composition remains an open research problem.

Demonstrated in published work

Parts of the foundation exist.

Programmable DNA scaffolds; synthetic molecular machines carrying out selective chemistry; electrically and magnetically actuated DNA devices; RNA-fueled motion; modeling tools for hybrid nucleic-acid nanostructures. These results are important but are not equivalent to a general molecular factory.

Scientifically unresolved

The integration gap is serious.

How to combine useful force and chemical selectivity with reliable placement, direct verification, recovery from defects, high throughput, and tolerable costs. Molecular state reporters, including spin-sensitive DNA systems, are promising avenues, not proven general workcell sensors.

Long-horizon hypothesis

Programmable matter remains the destination.

Atomically precise systems, adaptive artificial substrates, and ultimately self-maintaining structures could become accessible if verified molecular operations can be repeated and composed. No present experimental result establishes that such general capability is achievable or economical.

10 / Who belongs in this field?

An uncommon coalition around a common problem.

The missing links are often between disciplines rather than inside them. The field should create an incentive for people who would otherwise never share experimental standards to work on the same measurable task.

A possible first grantmaking program

Fund the interfaces between specialties.

Start with small, falsifiable bets. Let applicants define a molecular operation, the outcome they will independently measure, and the observation that would invalidate the approach.

  • Open calls that do not rely on institutional prestige
  • Clear review criteria and conflict-of-interest recusals
  • Support for unconventional or overlooked researchers
  • Reusable measurements and publication of negative results
  • Independent replication and make-or-break decisions
